What is viticulture?	The cultivation of grapevines (grape growing).	CMS_Intro Basics_Viticulture
What is vinification?	The process of making wine.	CMS_Intro Basics_Winemaking
What is Vitis vinifera?	The European grapevine species used for nearly all fine wine.	CMS_Intro Basics_Viticulture
What is phylloxera?	A root-feeding insect that devastated European vineyards in the late 1800s. Controlled by grafting vinifera vines onto American rootstocks.	CMS_Intro Basics_Viticulture
What is the order of the vine's annual growth cycle?	Dormancy, budbreak, flowering, fruit set, veraison, harvest.	CMS_Intro Basics_Viticulture
What is veraison?	The onset of ripening, when grapes change color and soften.	CMS_Intro Basics_Viticulture
What is Botrytis cinerea (noble rot)?	A fungus that shrivels grapes and concentrates sugar and acid. Used for sweet wines like Sauternes and Tokaji Aszu.	CMS_Intro Basics_Viticulture
What is must?	Freshly crushed grape juice, including skins, seeds, and sometimes stems.	CMS_Intro Basics_Winemaking
What does yeast convert during alcoholic fermentation?	Sugar into alcohol (ethanol) and carbon dioxide, plus heat.	CMS_Intro Basics_Winemaking
Where does red wine get its color?	From contact with the grape skins (maceration). The juice of most red grapes is clear.	CMS_Intro Basics_Winemaking
How is rose typically made?	Red grapes with brief skin contact (a few hours to a couple of days), then fermented like a white wine.	CMS_Intro Basics_Winemaking
Where does tannin in wine come from?	Grape skins, seeds, and stems, plus oak barrels.	CMS_Intro Basics_Winemaking
What is malolactic conversion?	Bacteria convert sharp malic acid into softer lactic acid. Can add buttery notes (diacetyl), common in Chardonnay.	CMS_Intro Basics_Winemaking
What is chaptalization?	Adding sugar to the must to increase the final alcohol level.	CMS_Intro Basics_Winemaking
What are lees?	Dead yeast cells that settle after fermentation. Aging on the lees (sur lie) adds texture and bready notes.	CMS_Intro Basics_Winemaking
What is batonnage?	Stirring the lees to add richness and texture.	CMS_Intro Basics_Winemaking
What is carbonic maceration?	Fermenting whole, uncrushed grapes in a CO2-rich environment. Produces fruity, low-tannin wines; associated with Beaujolais.	CMS_Intro Basics_Winemaking
What is a fortified wine?	Wine with added grape spirit (brandy), such as Port, Sherry, and Madeira.	CMS_Intro Basics_Winemaking
What is the traditional method of sparkling wine?	A second fermentation in the bottle. Used for Champagne, Cava, Franciacorta, and Cremant.	CMS_Intro Basics_Sparkling
What is the tank (Charmat) method?	A second fermentation in a pressurized tank. Used for Prosecco.	CMS_Intro Basics_Sparkling
What is liqueur de tirage?	A mixture of wine, sugar, and yeast added to start the second fermentation in the bottle.	CMS_Intro Basics_Sparkling
What is riddling (remuage)?	Gradually turning and tilting bottles to move yeast sediment into the neck.	CMS_Intro Basics_Sparkling
What is disgorgement?	Removing the frozen plug of yeast sediment from the bottle neck.	CMS_Intro Basics_Sparkling
What is dosage?	A mix of wine and sugar added after disgorgement that sets the final sweetness.	CMS_Intro Basics_Sparkling
Sparkling wine sweetness levels, driest to sweetest?	Brut Nature, Extra Brut, Brut, Extra Dry, Sec, Demi-Sec, Doux.	CMS_Intro Basics_Sparkling
